# ad-1

Played at `/?marketing=ad-1`. The default call to action is `cta=install`. `cta=demo`, `cta=ea`, and `cta=none` change only the end card. `part=pain` stops after the dull sea. `part=pitch` starts at the switch. `part=cta` starts on the end card.

Lines in quotes are on screen or spoken. **VO** is the narrator (ElevenLabs Eleven v4, voice Sienna). **Clerk** is the product's own voice (the live agent's ElevenLabs voice), speaking to the shopper. **Shopper** speaks one line out loud in the pitch (voice Jessica). All recorded lines are listed in `public/promo/voice/film-lines.json` and generated by `scripts/generate-film-voice.mjs`; the picture waits for each narrator line, so the voice sets the pace. The VO never overlaps the clerk or the shopper: it speaks over the silent work (typing, searching, a page opening), never over a spoken line.

Text on screen carries the story for a muted viewer (landing autoplay). The VO never reads it out; it adds the why in a fuller sentence, or stays quiet.

The film says "chatbot" and "sales agent". "Salesperson" is only the struck word in the reveal, and the narrator's line about a physical store. Lead with the pain, then the outcome (sub-benefit), then the feature that makes it possible; never feature-first. No strawman jokes about chatbots: the chatbot's failure is shown factually (a generic answer, a link to a policy page) and said plainly.

Two palettes, and the switch is the only transition between them. Before the knob flips, the picture is greyscale: white field, white cards, light grey product tiles, dark grey type. The products are the same renders as the warm half, with the colour taken out. LOST is a mid grey. The chatbot uses the same neutral greys as the store. From the flip onward the field is a quiet warm grey, and the skeletons, rules, and type stay neutral grey so they do not share a hue with Bizmis orange. Product tiles are stone, sand, blush, sage, or warm grey. Each product photo is the full studio render, backdrop and soft shadow included, never a cutout. Cold, the whole photo is greyscale. Warm, the whole photo takes the tile's tint, backdrop, product, and shadow together. Bizmis orange means the agent acted or the sale happened: the selection ring, the Add button as it fills, the cart badge, the SOLD veil and check, the switch burst, the clerk's shirt, the mark, and the end-card wordmark. The only orange type is the reveal's "salesagent." and the words "Sales agent".

---

The picture is 16:9, and the exported file is that same frame. A low-resolution preview is that same layout, scaled down. It changes sharpness only.

The desktop store is 16:10. The phone mockup is 9:19 and tall in the frame. The tablet mockup is 3:4.

## 1. Lost in the catalog (desktop, no help at all)

The first close-up is a laptop window, 16:10. In under a second it opens the way a Mac window unminimizes, with the store icon and a large "Your store" already on it; the title gives way to the catalog, and the store name and the cart fade in. The catalog fills the window: four columns, two full rows, more below. This store has no chat at all. The cursor opens a product, backs out, hovers a look-alike, opens it, backs out, scrolls, and drifts out of the window. A grey veil and "LOST" stamp the catalog.

**VO** `n-open`: "Every day, shoppers come to your store ready to buy."

**VO** `n-catalog`: "Some get lost in the catalog, with no one to help them choose."

## 2. The last doubt (phone, with a chatbot)

The camera pans right to a phone on the same table, already on a product page; question marks orbit the photo. A thumb taps the chatbot's circle (a plain grey "Chat" panel, no brand jokes). The shopper types: "What if she doesn't like it?" Three dots, then the bot: "Thanks for your question! For information about returns and exchanges, please see our Returns Policy page. Is there anything else I can help you with?" Buttons: Returns policy, Contact us. LOST stamps the page.

**VO** `n-doubt`: "Others get stuck on one last doubt, and a chatbot just points them to the FAQ."

## 3. The dull sea (scale)

The pullback starts on that phone and the sea of greyscale store mockups fades in around it (about a third with no chat, the rest with the chatbot). Every card stamps LOST at its own random moment. Over the tail of the glide, four beats, the scale of the loss:

"Unattended visits"
"cost you sales."
"Every day."
"And counting."

**VO** `n-leave`: "Unattended or poorly attended, they just leave, and you never find out why." The last two beats land after the line, in silence over the stamping sea.

## 4. The switch

A switch in the centre: "Typical chatbot" and "Sales agent". The knob flips to Sales agent, which moves to centre, grows, and bursts.

**VO** `n-physical` (as the switch comes up): "In a physical store, a salesperson would catch both."

## 5. The reveal

The Bizmis mark docks beside the avatar. **VO** `n-meet`: "Meet Bizmis." The headline "Your store salesperson." writes on; an orange line strikes "person" and the word rewrites as "salesagent." in Bizmis orange.

**VO** `n-agent` (over the strike): "Not a chatbot. Your store's sales agent."

## 6. One desktop, sold (an industry-agnostic gift)

One desktop window, the live Bizmis card in its corner. A chapter line sits in the margin above the window: the pain in grey, struck through in orange when the clerk resolves it and rewritten as the outcome (the reveal's own move). The clerk talks to the shopper, never to the viewer; its captions sit bottom centre inside the window. The shopper adds to cart; the clerk never does.

Chapter: "Lost in the catalog" → "Found the right one".

**VO** `n-lost` (over the shopper's typing and the search): "Lost in the catalog? It narrows the options and compares them, so they find the right one."

The shopper types: "I need a gift for my sister, under $50." The grid narrows to three (capsule on sage, sphere on apricot, rounded cube on stone).

**Clerk:** "Here are three she'd love. Let me compare them for you."

Three glass chips on each photo: rating, price, delivery, each a tick or a cross. The pick's are all orange and it keeps an orange ring.

**Clerk**, on its own: "This one's the best rated, and it's in your budget. It's the one."

Chapter: "Stuck on a doubt" → "Doubt cleared". The pick's page opens; question marks pop in around the photo.

**VO** `n-doubts`: "Stuck on a doubt? It answers on the spot, from your store's own details."

**Shopper**, out loud (a mic and live bars in the composer, the transcript filling in): "What if she doesn't like it?"

**Clerk:** "No problem. She can return it free within thirty days."

The question marks break into orange dust; the chapter line resolves; the shopper presses Add and the badge shows 1. The clerk looks for what goes with it; the match appears beside the pick.

**Clerk:** "Great choice. This one goes perfectly with it."

The shopper adds it too; the badge goes to 2; the orange veil and white check: SOLD.

## 7. The selling sea

The pullback starts on that same desktop, the cross-sell still on it and the widget still in the corner of the picture. The rest of the sea fades in around it as the camera pulls back, and no two cards near each other show the same screen. There is no center curtain. The frame stays full of mockup screens, with no empty stretches of field. Every card is "Your store". Most of them stamp "SOLD", each at its own random moment while on screen, never in rows. The cards stand thick off the floor. When a card sells it clicks up, higher still, and stays there, and one fine orange line drifts out from its edge and fades. The Bizmis widget on the other cards is caught mid-task: idle, talking, thinking, a wave, a nod, a salute, a bow, a thumbs-up, or the widget's own activity laser orbiting the avatar, with a grid, an eye, a book, or a cart on the tip. On the phone the catalog runs down the screen, and the home hero spans the width. On desktop the comparison sits clear of the corner widget. The cross-sell plus between the two products is large. While the sea is still moving and blurred, this line comes up and stays through the orange field, white, on one line, the Bizmis wordmark as its last word:

"Boost sales with [bizmis]"

**VO** `n-attended`: "Every shopper, attended. All day."

The sea washes to solid Bizmis orange.

## 8. Other stores, into the slot

"Boost sales with [bizmis]" holds on the orange field. The orange turns out to be the first store, The Apricot Theory, seen completely out of focus and filling the frame. The words fade; then the focus pulls in as the camera eases back, the orange clearing into the store's own golden colors, until its window sits in white space. One continuous move, no wipe. The mark is off during the pass. No clerk on screen. The window stays in place between stores. Each switch happens on the light: the backlight is at its dimmest, the store color changes in the dark, and the page dissolves through a slight lift of exposure and soft focus before the next store settles sharp. No color flood. Each card shows that store's real recording at the moment its clerk acts, silent, and the clerk's voice answers with one short confirmation: "Here are your options!", "There you go!", "We do have that!", "Added to your cart.", "Great choice!", "Here it is.", "Done!". The first two stores hold long enough to read, about three seconds each. After that each store is shorter than the last, so the pass gathers speed. A backlight hugs each card like LEDs on the back of a monitor and spills into a wide halo of the store's color, brightest and almost still in the middle of each store, sweeping faster and dimming toward the switch, drifting and breathing on its own. Above the frame, on one line: the sector on the left, heavy capitals filled with a gradient of the store color, the store name on the right in a glass chip. Sector order: Skincare & beauty, Consumer electronics, Clothing & apparel, Books & stationery, Gaming gear, Home & DIY, Car parts & accessories, Wine & spirits.

The wheel ends on a blank "Your store": an empty white store window with the Bizmis widget in its corner, under a Bizmis orange light. Then the camera dives into its white page, fast, until the window is gone and the frame is white, and the call to action appears there.

**VO** `n-any-store`: "Any store. Any catalog." The clerk's store confirmations never play while the narrator speaks.

## 9. End card: VO

**VO:** per `cta`.
- demo `see-it`: "See it in action."
- ea: five chunks, each driving its own visual so sound and picture stay in sync: `n-ea-1` "Join Early Access, open to the first fifty stores." (over the handwriting; the "first 50 stores only!" tag stamps as it ends), then one per tick: `n-ea-2` "Twelve hundred free credits." `n-ea-3` "Installs in one click." `n-ea-4` "Private until you go live." `n-ea-5` "And you shape what we build next."
- install `install-shopify`: "Install it on Shopify."
- none: silence.

Claims follow the Early Access docs: never "No credit card", never a public discount percentage, never "Sets itself up". "Private until you go live" is the theme app embed staying off until the merchant switches it on.

## 9. End card: by `cta`

The frames leave. The field stays white. Nothing is clickable. No window, button, cursor, glow, or glass. The wordmark is Bizmis orange, centred above the copy.

- `install` (default): eyebrow "Installs in one click." then the line "Install on Shopify". No URL.
- `demo`: the line "See it in action" and `bizmis.ai/demo`.
- `ea`: the Bizmis wordmark flies in from the camera, large and soft, and settles centred above the copy. After a pause, "Install now" flies in the same way and settles on the line with the Shopify bag. The bag sits on the same baseline as the words, slightly smaller than the capitals, with a small gap before the text. Under it, "for Early Access benefits!" writes on in a handwritten face, Bizmis orange, tilted a little. The line stays centred. When the writing finishes, a white discount tag with orange type stamps in just under the end of the line: "first 50 stores only!", with "50 stores" in bold. Under that, a 2×2 grid of terms, each an orange tick drawing itself as the narrator says it: "1,200 free credits", "Installs in one click", "Private until you go live", "Shape what we build next". The words are a darker heading face. No URL. The metal shine plays on "Install now" only. The card is drawn at 1.6× so every term reads at a glance.
- `none`: the wordmark only.

The call-to-action line plays a metal shine. It fades up and holds ≥ 4 s.

`&vo=1` prints the marker name as a small debug caption. The list is `PROMO_VO` in `film-engine.js` (`window.__promoVo`). Narrator lines play through `speakNarrator(id)`, which logs the cue so the exporter mixes it and resolves when the line ends.

Sound design follows the picture: `promoSfx(id)` logs every event (stamps, clicks, sends, cart, ticks, strikes, the burst…); the exporter writes them to `markers.json` (`sfx`, plus every recorded line under `voice`), and the mixer lays each sound on its own event, so a retime never knocks the sound out of sync. Music is one composition at one tempo, its pain section the same motif kept muted; its drop is fitted to the switch burst.
